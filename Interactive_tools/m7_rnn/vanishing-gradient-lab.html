Module 7 · Recurrent Neural Networks for Time Series Forecasting

Vanishing and Exploding Gradients

A recurrent network learns by sending its error backward through time. At every step back, that signal is multiplied by roughly the same factor. A little below 1 and it vanishes. A little above 1 and it explodes. Change the weights and the length of the sequence, then see what an LSTM does differently.

1 · The same factor, again and again

Going back one step multiplies the signal by the slope of tanh times the recurrent weights. That factor is about the same at every step. 0.84 does not sound bad, but 0.84 multiplied by itself 50 times is less than 2 parts in 10,000.

2 · tanh is only half of the story

People blame tanh, because its slope is never more than 1. The weights are the other half, and they can push the other way. Make them twice as large and the signal grows instead of fading.

3 · Clipping fixes one side

For exploding gradients there is a simple patch: put a ceiling on the size of the update. For vanishing gradients there is no patch. You cannot clip a signal back into existence.

4 · What the LSTM changes

Along the cell state, one step back multiplies the signal by one thing: the forget gate. Near 1, the signal survives. Half closed, it dies faster than in the simple RNN. An LSTM does not remove the problem. It gives the gradient a path and lets training keep it open. See the gates move in the LSTM conveyor belt.

How it is measured: a recurrent layer with 16 units and 1 input feature reads a sequence of random numbers (mean 0, standard deviation 1). Starting weights follow Keras: orthogonal recurrent weights (then multiplied by the slider), Glorot uniform input weights, zero biases, and for the LSTM a forget gate bias set by its slider (Keras uses 1). The error signal starts at the last step, and its size is read at every earlier step: the gradient of the last state with respect to that step's input, averaged over many sequences and several sets of starting weights. The update size is the norm of the full gradient with respect to the weights of one simple RNN. These are networks at the start of training, before any learning.
