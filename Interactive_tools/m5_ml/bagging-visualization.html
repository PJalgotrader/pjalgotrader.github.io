Bagging the Airline Passengers

Monthly international airline passengers, 1949 to 1960, in thousands. Each tree forecasts next month from the last three months and the calendar month. One deep tree overreacts to quirks in the history. Bagging trains many trees on bootstrap resamples of 1950 to 1958 and averages them. Then we score the forecasts on 1959 and 1960, which no tree ever saw.

1 · Bootstrap the rows

After building lag features, every month becomes one row: its last three months and its calendar month in, its value out. Bagging draws those rows with replacement. Some months show up twice, about a third never show up, so every tree learns a slightly different history.

2 · Average the forecasts

If each tree's forecast has variance σ²T and trees are correlated by ρ, the average has variance ρσ²T + (1 − ρ)σ²T/B. More trees shrink the second term only. That is why the curve flattens instead of reaching zero.

3 · OOB is optimistic for time series

An out of bag month still has training months right before and after it, so OOB error is an interpolation score. For a forecast you want a later holdout, or time series cross validation with an expanding window.

4 · Trees can't extrapolate

A tree predicts averages of values it has seen. Switch the target to raw counts and every 1960 forecast is stuck below the 1958 peak, no matter how many trees you bag. Modeling the seasonal log change removes the trend, so the trees only learn growth rates, and the forecast rebuilds the level.

Data: Box and Jenkins monthly international airline passengers, January 1949 to December 1960, thousands. Rows start in April 1950 so every lag exists. Training rows run through December 1958; the 24 months of 1959 and 1960 are forecast one step ahead, each using the actual previous months. Seasonal log change is dt = log Pt − log Pt−12, roughly year over year growth, and the forecast is P̂t = Pt−12 · ed̂t. Trees are CART regression trees with no pruning. A plain row bootstrap ignores the order of time; a block bootstrap, which resamples runs of neighboring months, is a common fix. Bagging builds deep trees in parallel to cut variance. Boosting builds shallow trees in sequence to cut bias.
