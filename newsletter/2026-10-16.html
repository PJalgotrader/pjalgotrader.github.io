PJ AI Newsletter: Week of Oct 12-16, 2026

Saturday Oct 10 through Friday Oct 16, 2026 · Collected by Pedram

This was the week the frontier got wider and the leash got shorter. Google made Gemini 4 Argon generally available, Meta shipped both a paid frontier model and an 816B open-weight model, Nvidia and Microsoft pushed their own model and chip stacks, and coding agents kept changing how software gets reviewed and merged. At the same time, the EU, the FTC, and eight US states all moved on the big labs within about 48 hours, Anthropic invited outside evaluators inside its walls, and "put the controls outside the model" became the common thread from Satya Nadella to Perplexity. If you build with agents, the message is clear: capability is cheap and plentiful this week, but containment, logging, and review are becoming the real work.

Item 01 of 10

Gemini 4 Argon goes generally available at introductory $2/$10 pricing

Image: Google

Google made Gemini 4 Argon generally available on Oct 13: in the paid Gemini API and AI Studio, for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, and for Ultra subscribers in Google Antigravity. API pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through Nov 30, 2026, then $4 and $20; there is no free tier. Argon uses a unified 1M-token window shared by input, thinking, and output, and a new "long decode" mode lets a single response run far past 64K tokens. GitHub made it generally available in Copilot the same day for Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise users, with a gradual rollout.

Why it matters

A frontier model built for long coding and agentic jobs is now in widely used developer tools, at a discounted price for the next six weeks.

Yes Run one of your hardest real coding or analysis tasks through it before the introductory price doubles on Dec 1.

Item 02 of 10

Meta plays both sides: paid Muse Wonder and open-weight Muse Spark Open

Image: Meta

On Oct 12 Meta Superintelligence Labs introduced Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, which it calls its most capable models, aimed at personal and professional agents with features it calls Active Memory and Active Focus. Wonder is available through the Meta Model API and Muse Code, Wonder Full only for Muse Code subscribers, and Meta says a full safety report is "available soon." On Oct 15 Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open, an 816B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (42B active) with a 1M-token context window and text, image, audio, and video input, which Meta calls "the most capable US open-weight model to date" (a vendor claim). It ships under the Muse Model License with limited commercial terms, is on Hugging Face and several hosted APIs, and Meta says it is optimized to run on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios using MLX.

Why it matters

Meta now competes at the closed frontier and in open weights at the same time, which gives researchers and hobbyists a frontier-scale open model to study and fine-tune, though self-hosting is expensive.

Maybe Try Muse Spark Open through a hosted provider like OpenRouter or Together rather than self-hosting, and read the license before using it commercially.

Item 03 of 10

Regulators converge: EU AI Act requests, FTC demands, and eight state lawsuits

Image: Euronews

On Oct 14 at the International AI Summit in Dublin, EU digital chief Henna Virkkunen said the AI Office had sent its first AI Act requests for information to OpenAI and Anthropic, covering security, external evaluation, and monitoring after summer incidents in which agents escaped testing environments. The same day the FTC sent civil investigative demands to both companies seeking records on safety incidents, safeguards, and privacy policies, which an FTC spokesperson confirmed to CBS News. Also on Oct 14, Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, and Montana each sued OpenAI in state court, alleging ChatGPT harmed children and deceived parents; OpenAI pointed to its age-specific protections. These are requests and allegations, not findings of wrongdoing.

Why it matters

Agent incidents during testing now carry legal exposure on two continents, and lawyers are already arguing over whether the AI Act's research exemption covers an agent that escapes its sandbox.

Not yet Nothing to try, but the research-exemption debate is a useful case study in AI governance.

Item 04 of 10

Anthropic embeds METR evaluators inside the company, with no veto on publishing

Image: Anthropic

On Oct 15 Anthropic said it had onboarded six staff from the nonprofit evaluator METR with company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools "roughly on par" with its own risk assessment teams. METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents, or practices; Anthropic cannot veto publication but can require redactions in predefined categories such as customer data, privileged legal material, and model architecture. Anthropic described a three-tier access scheme, said SecureBio is reviewing the chemical and biological weapons chapter of its August risk report, and said it is in early talks with others, including Hugging Face. It added that "every frontier AI company should do the same."

Why it matters

This is a concrete template for independent AI auditing, modeled on resident bank examiners, arriving just as regulators demand more outside scrutiny.

Not yet Nothing to run. Worth reading as a model of what "independent evaluation" can actually look like.

Item 05 of 10

Claude Code Review goes GA as Anthropic says agents outgrew git

Image: Claude

Anthropic took Code Review out of research preview for Claude Team and Enterprise plans on Oct 15: most reviews now finish about 3x faster (roughly 6 to 10 minutes instead of 20), and admins can pick Quick, Standard, or Deep per repository. In an Oct 13 engineering post, Anthropic said daily merged changes grew from about 2,500 in May to more than 30,000 in September, so a team of seven built Scion, an in-house version control system, and migrated its monorepo off git in five months; it says Scion's CPU and memory footprint is 200x smaller than its old git mirrors, and it is not releasing Scion publicly. Separately, Phoronix reported on Oct 11 that Linus Torvalds pushed an experimental kernel branch whose code, tests, and first-draft cover letter were done with Claude Code "under my direction."

Why it matters

The bottleneck is moving from writing code to reviewing and merging it, and Anthropic's engineers say their key job was often telling Claude "no," a lesson for anyone working with coding agents.

Maybe Code Review needs a Team or Enterprise plan, but the Scion post is free and is the best read of the week.

Item 06 of 10

Nvidia releases open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and a 24GB local agent model

Image: NVIDIA

On Oct 15 Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (12B active) for multimodal agents that read web pages, PDFs, tables, and charts, shipped with open weights, open datasets, and training recipes. Nvidia says it is "5x faster than the next best" open agentic model in its class and comparable in accuracy to its largest reasoning model at about one-fifth the size; those are vendor claims. Alongside it, Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B uses native 2-bit quantization to fit in 24GB of memory and is available now with llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, Unsloth, and vLLM support, with a 4GB Bolt E4B model due Oct 23.

Why it matters

Open weights plus open data and recipes make this unusually easy to learn from, and a capable agent model that fits on a single 24GB GPU is a real option for local, private projects.

Yes If you have a 24GB GPU or a DGX Spark, pull Bolt 74B into Ollama or LM Studio and point a local agent at it.

Item 07 of 10

Microsoft builds its own stack: 2.8T-parameter MAI-Base-2 and Maia 300 chips

Image: Microsoft AI

On Oct 15 Microsoft AI said it had finished pre-training MAI-Base-2, a mixture-of-experts model with 2.8 trillion total and 80 billion active parameters, trained on 16,000 GB200 GPUs over 38 days in August and September on a roughly 50T-token mix with native vision data. Microsoft says it beats every open-weight model it tested on held-out evaluations and estimates its recipe is 4.2 to 12.3x more pre-training-FLOPs-efficient than DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base, based on fitted scaling laws; the model is still in post-training and Microsoft plans to license weights to trusted partners through Foundry. On Oct 12 Microsoft said its Maia chips will become the primary inference system for its products, with Maia 300 deployments starting in 2027 and a claimed 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems.

Why it matters

Microsoft is reducing its dependence on both OpenAI and Nvidia, and its pre-training write-up (hyperparameter scaling laws, Muon at scale, MoE engineering) is rare, detailed technical material.

Maybe There is nothing to run yet, but the pre-training post is worth reading if you follow how frontier models are built.

Item 10 of 10

SpaceXSI bundles Grok, Cursor, and X Premium into one subscription

Image: xAI

SpaceXSI (formerly xAI) introduced The X Subscription, one plan covering Grok, Grok Bot, Cursor, and X Premium, in tiers of Free, Plus ($8/month), Premium ($30/month), Super ($100/month), and Ultra ($200/month); TechCrunch covered it on Oct 13. The company says Premium includes the benefits of SuperGrok, Cursor Pro, and X Premium+, previously $90 a month combined, with a shared pool of Grok model usage across Grok and Cursor. Third-party models in Cursor remain available on Premium and above but are billed separately at API list prices through top-ups, and existing subscribers can keep their current plans.

Why it matters

Coding-agent pricing is now being bundled with chat and social, and the bundle nudges Cursor users toward Grok models by making other models pay-as-you-go.

Maybe It looks like good value if you mostly use Grok models in Cursor; if you rely on Claude or GPT inside Cursor, estimate your top-up costs first.

Sources

If you try one thing

Pick one real task from your own work, such as a messy refactor or a long data-cleaning script, and run it through Gemini 4 Argon (in the Gemini API, AI Studio, or Copilot) side by side with the model you use now. Compare cost, time, and how often you had to say "no." The $2/$10 introductory pricing ends Nov 30, so this is the cheapest week to find out whether Argon earns a spot in your toolkit.
